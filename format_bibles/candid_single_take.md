# Format Bible: Candid Single Take

Source: real production brief ("โทรศัพท์เครื่องนี้ของใครคะ?", 2026-08-31).
The defining trait: it must read as footage that just happened to capture
her, not content she's making — the opposite instinct from every other
format bible in this folder, all of which assume some awareness of camera.

```
Camera:
- One continuous take, no [Shot N] cuts — the whole beat, setup through
  twist, plays in a single unbroken shot
- Fixed or steady handheld, never shaky-vlog handheld
- Medium-wide, waist-up or wider — enough room to see her whole
  posture/setting, not a tight close-up

Eyes:
- NEVER looks at the lens, not even once, not even on the punchline —
  this is the one hard rule that makes the format work. Gaze goes to the
  street, to the middle distance, down to the phone only when picking it
  up/checking it. Breaking this to glance at camera collapses the
  "accidentally filmed" premise the whole piece depends on.

Body:
- Calm, settled, low-energy physical presence — she is NOT performing;
  small idle gestures (hair touch, tapping the table, adjusting bangs)
  read as genuine idle behavior during a phone call, not staged business
- Reactions still need specific physical tells per naturalism rules, just
  restrained ones (a confused head-tilt, not a big cartoonish double-take)

Delivery:
- Flat/calm/matter-of-fact delivery throughout, even on objectively
  wild lines — the entire comedic engine is the mismatch between how
  extreme the content of a line is and how bored/normal her tone is
- Never let the tone spike into excitement or showing off; the moment it
  reads as bragging, the joke is dead

Setting:
- Everyday public place (street food stall, waiting room, transit stop)
  with faint ambient life in the background (traffic, distant chatter) —
  never staged/empty; the mundane backdrop is part of the "candid" sell

Pacing:
- Real phone-call pacing: listening beats (no dialogue) are as important
  as speaking beats — every listening beat still needs a numeric duration
  even with no line attached
```

## Common Failure Patterns
- Any direct-to-lens glance, including "just for the punchline" — this
  format has zero exceptions to the no-eye-contact rule, unlike
  `microphone_talk.md` which reserves eye contact for punchlines.
- Playing the escalating asks (bigger purchase each time) with rising
  excitement instead of flat calm — undercuts the "this is just a normal
  Tuesday for me" comedic premise the acting direction explicitly calls
  for.
- Treating listening silence as empty/still — needs the same numeric
  duration + small physical tell as a spoken beat.

## Best Suited For
`comedy` — single-scene misdirection/reveal pieces built on a flat,
deadpan delivery mismatched against escalating or absurd content.
