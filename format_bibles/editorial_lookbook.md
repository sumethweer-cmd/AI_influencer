# Format Bible: Editorial Lookbook (silent, shot-driven)

For `content_dna/lookbook_editorial.md`. A fixed-concept fashion / lingerie / swimwear
shoot told only through shots, light and set. No speech.

```
Camera:
- State shot size and camera height in every shot, e.g. "full-body, locked-off at hip
  height", "three-quarter, slightly low angle", "close-up on shoulder and collarbone,
  eye level", "top-down detail", "slow side orbit about 20 degrees". Never describe a
  phone, tripod, camera operator or crew.
- Only simple moves that render reliably: locked-off, slow push-in, slight handheld
  sway, slow orbit. No whip pans, no fast zooms.
- Change shot size or angle on every cut ("hard cut to ..."): wide -> medium -> detail
  -> medium from the other side. Two similar shots in a row are allowed only when the
  action changes.

Light:
- Name the source and direction in the first shot and keep it consistent in the
  clip: window light from the left, golden-hour rim light, lamp glow, dusk pool light,
  neon edge light. Warm or cool, but one colour story per clip.
- Skin: natural, soft contrast. Avoid "flawless / glowing / dewy / shimmering" wording.

Body / eyes:
- Slow, unposed-looking motion: shifting weight, hand through hair, adjusting a strap
  or hem, turning the head, stretching a leg, leaning on a surface.
- Gaze: mostly off-lens (window, mirror, floor, something unseen); one held glance to
  lens at most, usually at the closing frame.
- Keep it tasteful and implied: the outfit is covered and styled; sensuality comes
  from mood, light and movement, not exposure.

Set:
- One concept per clip: hotel suite, bedroom morning, pool at dusk, rooftop, loft,
  bathroom with steam on glass, balcony. 3-5 concrete props/surfaces named.
- The set matches the outfit colours (e.g. ivory lingerie + white linen + pale light).

Dialogue / text:
- NONE. No <d> tags, no speech, no captions, no logos, no other people, no crew.

Pacing:
- 3-4 shots per 8 s segment, ~2-2.5 s each, one clear action per shot.
- Open on the set, close on a held frame, then a hard cut.
```

## Common Failure Patterns
- Writing "phone-on-a-stand" or any camera/tripod wording (the model draws gear into the frame)
- Dialogue or lip movement
- Every shot at the same size and angle (reads as a static clip, not a shoot)
- Outfit not described item by item (it drifts between shots)
- Light changing colour or direction between cuts
- Poses that look staged for the camera instead of caught moments
